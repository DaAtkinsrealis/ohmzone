01 · Reference

Grounding system design — IEEE 80-2013

This page opens every generated report. It sets out why a grounding grid is checked, and the formulas this tool uses.

Why it is checked

During a ground fault, fault current flowing through the grounding grid raises the potential of the whole substation yard (ground potential rise, GPR). A person standing on the yard can then be exposed to a touch voltage — between a grounded structure and their feet — or a step voltage — between their two feet. IEEE Std 80-2013 gives the tolerable limits for both, based on the current a human body can withstand for the fault-clearing time. A grid that passes the resistance check alone can still fail the touch-voltage check.

What this tool checks: grid resistance (Sverak), mesh (touch) voltage and step voltage against the tolerable limits, ground potential rise, and a single-rod Dwight check for reference.

Soil resistivity is the single most sensitive input — a measured Wenner four-pin survey of the site always takes precedence over the representative presets in this tool.

The formulas this tool applies

QuantityFormula (IEEE Std 80-2013)
Single rod — DwightR = ρ / (2πL) · [ln(4L/d) − 1]
Grid resistance — Sverak (§14.2)Rg = ρ · [1/LT + 1/√(20A) · (1 + 1/(1 + h·√(20/A)))]
Surface-layer derating (§7.2)Cs = 1 − 0.09 · (1 − ρ/ρs) / (2·hs + 0.09)
Tolerable body current (§8)IB = 0.116/√ts (50 kg)  |  0.157/√ts (70 kg)
Tolerable touch / step (§8)Etol = (1000 + 1.5·Cs·ρs) · IB  |  Estep = (1000 + 6·Cs·ρs) · IB
Mesh voltage (§14.5)Em = ρ · Km · Ki · Ig / LM
Step voltage (§14.5)Es = ρ · Ks · Ki · Ig / LS
Ground potential riseGPR = Ig · Rg

Where ρ is soil resistivity (Ω·m), ρs surface-layer resistivity (Ω·m), hs surface-layer thickness (m), LT total buried conductor length including rods (m), A grid area (m²), h burial depth (m), Ig fault current (A), ts clearing time (s), and Km / Ks / Ki are the IEEE 80 geometric factors computed from the grid layout.
