01 · Reference

Voltage drop under CSA C22.1

This page opens every generated report. It sets out why voltage drop is checked, and the formula this tool uses.

Why it's checked

Excess voltage drop starves connected equipment of the voltage it was designed for — motors run hot and lose torque, lighting dims, electronic loads misbehave, and heating elements under-perform. CSA C22.1 (the Canadian Electrical Code, Part I) addresses this through Rule 8-102 and the associated Appendix B note.

Appendix B guidance to Rule 8-102 (informative, not a mandatory rule): voltage drop should not exceed 3% on either the feeder or the branch circuit alone, and should not exceed a combined 5% from the service/supply point to the farthest point of utilization.

Because it sits in the Code's informative appendix, an authority having jurisdiction can apply stricter project-specific limits — always confirm the target percentage against the governing specification before relying on this tool's default.

The formula this tool applies

Voltage drop is calculated from conductor resistance (R) and reactance (X), current, one-way circuit length, and the load's power factor angle θ:

SystemFormula
DCVd = 2 × I × L × R
AC — single phaseVd = 2 × I × L × (R·cosθ + X·sinθ)
AC — three phaseVd = √3 × I × L × (R·cosθ + X·sinθ)

Where I is load current (A), L is one-way conductor length (km), and R/X come from the conductor's resistance/reactance table for the chosen material and temperature rating (per the methodology of CSA C22.1 Appendix D, Table D3). Percentage drop is Vd ÷ Vsystem × 100.
